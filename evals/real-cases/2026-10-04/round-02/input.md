# Darjeeling tea — disclosed evidence partition (real case)
Decision frame: early-2000s protection/enforcement strategy, restricted to the source's contemporaneous information.
Primary: WTO, S.C. Srivastava, Protecting the Geographical Indication for Darjeeling Tea, Managing the Challenges of WTO Participation, case16. https://www.wto.org/English/res_e/booksp_e/casestudies_e/case16_e.htm
HTML sections I–III; references Tea Board papers presented 9–11 July2003. Publication collection is 2005, but narrative retains pending GI status. Treat as historical snapshot, not current law. Retrieved 2026-10-04.

Source-derived paraphrases:
- Darjeeling had an established quality/flavour reputation and substantial exports, including Europe and Japan.
- From February2000, compulsory dealer licensing and production/sale information supported authentic-export certification; blending with other origins was prohibited.
- Garden-invoice records supported consignment certificates; customs checked certificates before exports left India.
- Tea Board and producer association sought consumer authenticity and return of brand value to the industry/workers.
- Overseas misuse raised costly enforcement choices. A watch agency monitored attempted registrations; negotiations and legal challenges had mixed results.
- Source reports approximately US$200,000 protection costs over four years, excluding administration, personnel and monitoring systems.

Limits: consumer willingness-to-pay and worker benefit not measured here. Domestic exit control does not prove overseas retail integrity. Do not assume legal registration equals purchase motivation or benefit sharing. Modern legal advice is out of scope. Later observational outcomes withheld.
